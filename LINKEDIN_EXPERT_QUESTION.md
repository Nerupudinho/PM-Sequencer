# Question for LinkedIn Expert: Post Engagement Strategy

## Context & Background

I've built a **free educational platform** called "PM Sequence" that helps Product Managers learn through curated video sequences. Here's what it does:

**What the Platform Is:**
- A free, no-signup web platform for mid-to-senior PMs
- Curates and sequences YouTube videos from the "How I AI" podcast
- Organizes videos into problem-focused learning paths (e.g., "Turn meetings into momentum")
- Adds contextual guidance for each video ("watch for this")
- Uses YouTube's official embed API (videos remain on YouTube, not downloaded/re-hosted)
- Completely free - no monetization, no paywall

**The Value I'm Adding:**
- Curation: Selecting relevant videos from a large podcast library
- Sequencing: Organizing videos into logical learning paths
- Context: Adding guidance on what to focus on in each video
- Problem-focus: Grouping content by specific PM problems users face

## The LinkedIn Post Situation

I want to share this platform on LinkedIn, but I'm unsure about the best approach for directing people to it. I have two options:

### Option A: Comment + DM Pattern
**Post would say:**
> "I've built a free curated platform for mid to senior PMs to upskill themselves that sequences videos from How I AI podcast. If you're curious to try it, please comment 'Product Monk' and DM me."

**What happens:**
- Users comment "Product Monk"
- I send them the link via DM
- Creates visible engagement (comments) on the post

### Option B: Direct Link
**Post would say:**
> "I've built a free curated platform for mid to senior PMs to upskill themselves that sequences videos from How I AI podcast. Try it here: [direct link]"

**What happens:**
- Users click the link directly
- No comment requirement
- Less visible engagement on the post itself

## My Questions for the LinkedIn Expert

1. **Engagement Manipulation Concerns:**
   - Does asking users to comment a specific phrase ("Product Monk") and DM violate LinkedIn's policies on engagement manipulation?
   - Will LinkedIn suppress or penalize posts that use this pattern?
   - Is this considered similar to "engagement pods" or other manipulation tactics LinkedIn discourages?

2. **Link in Post vs. DM:**
   - Does including a direct link in the post help or hurt reach/algorithm performance?
   - Are posts with external links penalized by LinkedIn's algorithm?
   - Is the "DM for link" approach better for algorithm performance, or is it just a way to avoid link penalties?

3. **Best Practice Recommendation:**
   - For a free educational tool like this, what's the recommended approach that:
     - Maximizes legitimate reach
     - Doesn't trigger spam/manipulation filters
     - Follows LinkedIn's best practices
     - Still allows me to share the platform effectively

4. **Alternative Approaches:**
   - Would a comment like "DM me for the link" (without requiring a specific phrase) be better?
   - Should I use LinkedIn's native link preview feature?
   - Any other approaches you'd recommend for sharing free tools/educational content?

## Additional Context

- This is a personal project, not a business
- The platform is completely free (no monetization)
- I'm not collecting emails or building a list
- The content is curated from publicly available YouTube videos (properly embedded, not re-hosted)
- I'm transparent about the source (How I AI podcast) in my post

## What I'm Trying to Avoid

- Getting my post suppressed or penalized
- Violating LinkedIn's terms of service
- Being seen as spammy or manipulative
- Hurting my account's reach/reputation

## What I'm Trying to Achieve

- Share a valuable free resource with the PM community
- Get legitimate engagement from people who are genuinely interested
- Build my reputation as someone who contributes to the community
- Follow LinkedIn's best practices

**Thank you for your guidance!** I want to make sure I'm doing this the right way and not accidentally triggering any penalties or appearing manipulative.
